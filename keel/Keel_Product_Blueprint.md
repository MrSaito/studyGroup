# KEEL — Product Blueprint
### A curriculum runner for goals that take months

**One line:** Keel turns any long plan into one small thing to do today, re-flows the plan when life happens, and makes sure a missed week never becomes a quit.

**Working name:** Keel (the part of a ship that keeps it on course). Rename freely.

---

## 1. The honest premise

An app cannot manufacture motivation. What it can do is attack the five specific mechanisms that turn "I'm following a plan" into "I stopped":

| Failure mechanism | What actually happens | What most apps do wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgetting** | No cue at the right moment; the plan lives in a document you stop opening | Generic 8am notification everyone swipes away |
| **Boredom** | Same activity daily, no visible progress on a 9-month horizon | Confetti and badges that stop working by week 3 |
| **Lapse → abandonment** | Miss 3 days, face a pile-up of "overdue" items, feel shame, never reopen | Streak counters that make a single miss feel like total loss |
| **Rigidity** | Plan assumes a life you don't have; one bad week and the schedule is fiction | Static schedules that stack missed tasks into an impossible backlog |
| **Isolation** | Nobody notices if you stop | Public leaderboards that motivate the top 5% and demoralize the rest |

Keel's entire design is five mechanisms, five countermeasures. Everything else is secondary.

**What the evidence broadly supports** (behaviour-change literature, no exaggerated claims):
- Implementation intentions ("At [time], in [place], I will [action]") measurably increase follow-through versus vague intentions.
- Streaks work until they break; the break is a common quit point. Forgiveness mechanics (streak freezes, consistency-over-window metrics) reduce abandonment after a lapse.
- Social accountability to a small known group beats anonymous leaderboards for long goals.
- Fresh-start moments (new week, new month, after a lapse) are natural re-entry points if the re-entry step is small.
- Visible progress toward a distant goal sustains effort better than abstract points.

---

## 2. Positioning

**Not** a habit tracker (Streaks, Habitify), a gamified to-do list (Habitica), a focus timer (Forest), or a note tool (Notion). Those handle daily habits or single sessions. Keel handles **sequenced, multi-month plans** — a 36-week curriculum, a 6-month exam prep, a 12-week rehab protocol, a book draft — where each day builds on the last and the risk is drift, not one missed session.

**Who it's for (initial):** self-learners following long structured plans — coding roadmaps, professional exam prep (FCPS, USMLE, CFA), language learning, fitness programs, writing projects. First launch plan: the 36-week AI Engineer roadmap, pre-loaded.

**Why it can win:** the plan format (ordered daily units with Learn/Do/Tip) plus elastic re-flow plus lapse recovery is a combination nobody ships. Habit apps can't represent sequence; project tools can't represent forgiveness.

## 3. Core mechanics (the engine)

### 3.1 Plans and units
- A **Plan** is an ordered list of **Units**. A unit is one session: title, estimated minutes, `learn` (short text/link), `do` (the task), `tip`, optional `deliverable` (what to commit/produce), optional `checkpoint` flag (stage exit tests).
- Plans are importable from **Markdown or JSON** (the roadmap file format works directly), from **templates** (curated library), or generated by the AI coach from a goal description.
- Units have **types**: `learn`, `build`, `review`, `rest`. The scheduler enforces variety and rest.

### 3.2 The Today card
- Opening the app shows **one unit**: today's. Title, minutes, the `do` line, a Start button. Nothing else above the fold.
- **Start** opens the unit view with a timer, the learn/do/tip content, a "log 3 lines" field, and a **Done** button. Done triggers a 1-second progress animation showing where you are on the whole plan (e.g. "Day 47 of 216 — Stage 2, Week 11").
- **Not today** button (no shame copy): "Push to tomorrow" or "Swap for a 10-minute review". Both are legitimate outcomes, recorded as such.

### 3.3 Elastic scheduling (the anti-backlog rule)
- The schedule is a **projection, not a list of due dates**. Units are sequenced; dates are computed from your declared availability (days/week, minutes/day) and your actual completions.
- A missed day **shifts the whole sequence by one**; it never stacks two units on tomorrow. There is no "overdue" state anywhere in the app.
- Every week has a **buffer slot** (Day 5 in the roadmap format). Falling one day behind consumes the buffer silently.
- Weekly, the app shows the **projected finish date** and how it moved. That's the honest consequence of missing days — a later finish, not a red list.

### 3.4 Lapse recovery (the anti-quit rule)
- **Consistency score** replaces streaks: percentage of planned sessions completed over a rolling 28 days. A 2-day miss moves 100% to 93%, not to zero.
- After **3 consecutive missed days**, the next notification changes: "Ready for a 10-minute re-entry?" It opens a **Return unit** auto-generated from the last completed unit's content (a short recap + one small task). Completing it resumes the plan.
- After **7 missed days**, the app offers a **re-plan**: shrink the next two weeks to half-size units, or shift availability. It never shows how many days were missed on the home screen.
- After **21 missed days**, one message: "Your plan is still here. Want to restart at Stage X checkpoint, or archive it?" No further nudges until answered.
- Rule for all copy: **forgiveness by default; the shortest possible path to Start**.

### 3.5 Cue system (the anti-forgetting rule)
- Onboarding captures an **implementation intention**: time, place, trigger ("after dinner, at my desk, phone on silent"). This becomes the notification text: "After dinner, at your desk: Day 12 — Dictionaries (45 min)."
- Optional **calendar hold** written to Google/Apple Calendar for the session window.
- **Adaptive timing**: the app learns when you actually complete units and shifts the nudge toward that window.
- **Escalation ladder** (all gentle): push at planned time → second push 90 minutes later with a 10-minute alternative → next-day "buffer day" note. Never more than two pushes per day.
- **Pakistan/low-connectivity fallback**: optional SMS or WhatsApp nudge (via a gateway) for users who disable push or lose data connectivity; core app works fully offline.

### 3.6 Anti-boredom
- **Variety enforced by the scheduler**: no more than two `learn`-type units in a row without a `build` or `review`.
- **Progress map**: a single screen showing all stages as segments with completed units filled in. Zoomable to the week. This is the "distant goal made visible" screen; it is one tap from Today.
- **Retrieval quizzes** from the user's own logs: every Friday, three questions generated from their 3-line logs ("You wrote on Day 22 that `sorted()` returns a new list — what does `.sort()` return?"). Spaced repetition keeps early material alive and gives a sense of accumulation.
- **The "why" artefact**: onboarding asks for a 30-second voice note or 3 sentences on why this goal matters. It's replayed only in two places: the Return unit and the 7-day re-plan flow. Rarely shown = still meaningful.
- **Milestone moments**: stage completions and checkpoint passes get a shareable card (optional) and a short reflection prompt. Not confetti on every task.

### 3.7 Accountability
- **Pods**: 3–5 people on the same or similar plans. Pod screen shows each member's consistency score and current unit — no rankings, no streaks. A missed 3 days makes a member's card grey; pod members get a one-tap "nudge" (a pre-written friendly message, not free text, to prevent nagging).
- **Weekly check-in**: Sunday, 3 questions, auto-shared with the pod: what I finished, what stuck, next week's plan. Takes 2 minutes.
- **Commitment deposit** (premium, opt-in): stake an amount for a stage; hit the consistency threshold (e.g. 80%) and it's refunded; miss it and it goes to a charity you chose. This is a commitment device with real evidence behind it — and it's opt-in only.
- **Mentor mode**: a mentor (teacher, senior, coach) can view a mentee's progress and logs, leave comments on checkpoints, and approve exit tests. This is the B2B/education wedge.

### 3.8 The AI coach (premium)
- **Re-plan negotiation**: "I only have 4 hours this week" → the coach proposes a compressed week honouring unit sequence and rest rules; the user accepts or adjusts.
- **Stuck help**: from inside a unit, ask about the concept. The coach answers in the plan's context (it knows the unit's learn/do text) and refuses to just do the task for a `checkpoint` unit.
- **Return-unit generation**: builds the 10-minute re-entry from the last three completed units.
- **Weekly summary**: turns the week's logs into a paragraph the user can post or keep.
- **Guardrails**: the coach cannot mark units done, cannot skip checkpoints, and has a per-user daily token budget. All coach interactions are traced (Langfuse) and cost-capped — the same production discipline as the roadmap teaches.

## 4. Feature spec by tier

### Free
- 1 active plan (unlimited archived)
- Today card, unit view, timer, 3-line log
- Elastic scheduling, buffer days, projected finish date
- Consistency score and lapse recovery (Return units are templated, not AI-generated)
- Progress map
- Push notifications with implementation intention
- Plan import (Markdown/JSON) and the template library
- Full offline operation

### Premium — "Keel Plus"
- Unlimited active plans
- AI coach (re-plan, stuck help, generated Return units, weekly summaries)
- Pods (create/join) and pod nudges
- Commitment deposits
- Calendar sync and adaptive notification timing
- Retrieval quizzes from logs
- Analytics: best time of day, unit-type completion rates, projected-finish history
- Export of logs and progress (Markdown/CSV)

### Pricing
- **Pakistan:** PKR 499/month or PKR 3,999/year (roughly the price of one study guide; annual is the push).
- **International:** USD 4.99/month or USD 34.99/year.
- **Student/mentor programme:** mentors get a free seat per 5 paying mentees; institutions get per-seat pricing (later).
- **Creator marketplace (phase 3):** plan authors publish paid plans; Keel takes 20–30%. The AI Engineer roadmap is plan #1 and free.

### Payment reality
- Digital subscriptions sold **inside** the app must use Google Play Billing / Apple In-App Purchase (use RevenueCat to manage both). Store fees apply.
- For Pakistan, where cards are rare, offer **web checkout** via a local gateway (JazzCash/Easypaisa through a provider) and grant the entitlement server-side. This is compliant only if the purchase happens on the web and the app merely reads the entitlement — do not link to it from inside the iOS app; Android policy is more permissive but check current rules at launch.
- Plan for this from day one in the entitlement model: `subscriptions.source ∈ {play, appstore, web_pk}`.

## 5. Screens (MVP)

1. **Onboarding** (4 steps): goal → pick/import plan → availability (days/week, minutes/day) → implementation intention + "why" note.
2. **Today** — the single unit card; Start / Not today.
3. **Unit** — timer, learn/do/tip, log field, Done.
4. **Progress map** — stages → weeks → units; projected finish.
5. **Plan** — read-only sequence with the ability to reorder within a week or mark a unit as skipped-with-reason.
6. **Return** — the lapse re-entry flow (only appears when triggered).
7. **Weekly review** — Friday/Sunday prompt: three questions + retrieval quiz.
8. **Pod** — members, consistency, nudge, check-ins.
9. **Coach** — chat scoped to the current unit/plan.
10. **Settings** — availability, notifications, calendar, subscription, export, delete account.

Design rules: one primary action per screen; no red; no "overdue"; no counts of missed days on Today; every notification names the exact next action and its length.

## 6. Architecture

**Client:** Flutter (Android + iOS from one codebase; web later). Offline-first with a local SQLite database (`drift`) as the source of truth on device; a sync queue pushes changes when online. The app must be fully usable through a day of load-shedding or no data.

**Backend:** Supabase — Postgres (with RLS for per-user isolation), Auth (email/phone OTP; Google/Apple sign-in), Edge Functions for scheduling projections, notification fan-out, coach calls, and payment webhooks. Storage for the "why" voice notes.

**Sync:** row-versioned tables with `updated_at` and a device `change_log`; last-write-wins per row except `completions`, which are append-only (never lost). Conflicts on `schedule` are resolved by recomputing the projection server-side from completions + availability — the projection is derived data and never synced directly.

**Notifications:** FCM (Android) and APNs (iOS) via a scheduled Edge Function that computes each user's next nudge from their availability, adaptive timing, and lapse state. SMS/WhatsApp fallback via a gateway for opted-in users.

**Payments:** RevenueCat SDK for store subscriptions; a web checkout with a local Pakistani gateway; both write to a single `subscriptions` table via signed webhooks. Entitlements are checked server-side on every premium call.

**AI coach:** an Edge Function calling an LLM API with structured outputs; every call traced (Langfuse), errors to Sentry, per-user daily token budget enforced in Postgres, kill-switch env var. Prompts versioned. A small eval set (re-plan proposals must respect sequence and rest rules; coach must refuse to complete checkpoint units) runs in CI.

**Observability:** Sentry (client + functions), Langfuse (coach), PostHog or similar for product analytics with the events listed in §8.

**Security/privacy:** RLS on every table; logs and voice notes encrypted at rest; data export and full account deletion from day one; no third-party ad SDKs.

## 7. Data model (core tables)

- `users` (id, tz, availability_days, minutes_per_day, intention_time, intention_place, why_note_url)
- `plans` (id, owner_id, title, source ∈ {template, import, coach}, is_public, version)
- `units` (id, plan_id, seq, stage, week, type, title, est_minutes, learn, do, tip, deliverable, is_checkpoint)
- `enrollments` (id, user_id, plan_id, started_at, status, current_seq)
- `completions` (id, enrollment_id, unit_id, started_at, completed_at, minutes, outcome ∈ {done, swapped_review, pushed}, log_text) — append-only
- `schedule_projection` (enrollment_id, unit_id, projected_date) — derived, recomputed
- `lapse_events` (enrollment_id, detected_at, days_missed, resolution ∈ {returned, replanned, archived, pending})
- `pods` (id, name, plan_id nullable), `pod_members` (pod_id, user_id, role), `checkins` (pod_id, user_id, week, finished, stuck, next), `nudges` (from, to, at)
- `deposits` (user_id, enrollment_id, stage, amount, threshold, status ∈ {held, refunded, forfeited}, charity_id)
- `subscriptions` (user_id, tier, source, status, renews_at, provider_ref)
- `coach_sessions` (user_id, unit_id, trace_id, tokens_in, tokens_out, cost)
- `notification_prefs` (user_id, push, sms, whatsapp, quiet_hours, adaptive)

## 8. Metrics

**North star:** planned units completed per active user per week.

**Retention:** D1, D7, D30, D90 (the 90-day number is the one that matters for a months-long product).

**Behavioural (the ones that test the thesis):**
- **Lapse recovery rate**: % of users who complete a unit within 7 days of a 3-day lapse. Target ≥ 50%. If this is under 30%, the core bet has failed.
- **Notification → Start conversion** within 2 hours.
- **Plan completion at 12 weeks**: % of enrollments still active with consistency ≥ 60%.
- **Checkpoint pass rate** by stage (also feeds plan quality).
- **Pod effect**: 12-week retention of pod members vs solo users.

**Business:** free → premium conversion (target 3–5%), annual share of premium, churn, ARPU by region, coach cost per premium user (must stay under 20% of subscription price).

## 9. Build plan

### MVP (8 weeks, one developer with Claude Code)
- Weeks 1–2: Flutter shell, local DB, plan import (Markdown → units), Today/Unit/Progress screens, offline completions.
- Weeks 3–4: Supabase auth, sync, scheduling projection, buffer days, projected finish, push notifications with implementation intention.
- Weeks 5–6: consistency score, lapse detection, templated Return units, weekly review, retrieval quiz (rule-based from logs).
- Weeks 7–8: Sentry, analytics events, TestFlight/Play internal track, onboarding polish. Ship to a **closed cohort of 20–30 users following the AI Engineer roadmap**.

**MVP success criterion (8 weeks after cohort start):** ≥ 50% of the cohort still completing ≥ 3 units/week, and ≥ 50% lapse-recovery rate. If not met, fix the core loop before building premium features.

### Phase 2 (weeks 9–16): Premium
- RevenueCat + store subscriptions; web checkout for Pakistan; entitlement service.
- AI coach with traces, budgets, evals; generated Return units; re-plan negotiation.
- Pods, check-ins, nudges. Calendar sync. Adaptive notification timing.

### Phase 3 (months 5–8): Growth
- Template library with 10 curated plans (exam prep, fitness, writing, languages).
- Mentor mode; institutional pilots (a residency programme, a coaching centre).
- Creator marketplace. Commitment deposits (requires payment-out flow and charity partners — legal review first).

- **Seed cohort:** learners of the AI Engineer roadmap, recruited from the communities where it's shared. The plan and the app launch together; each sells the other.
- **Second wedge:** FCPS/exam-prep candidates in Pakistan via the same Facebook/WhatsApp channels that sell study guides — a 6-month exam plan pre-loaded, priced in PKR, paid via local gateway.
- **Mentor/institution channel:** a coaching centre or residency programme adopting Keel for its trainees is 20–100 seats at once and near-zero churn.
- **Content flywheel:** weekly summaries and milestone cards are shareable by design; each one links to the plan.

## 11. Risks and honest pushback

1. **Habit/productivity apps have brutal retention.** Most lose 80%+ of users within a month. Keel's thesis is that sequence + forgiveness + pods change that curve for long plans. Treat that as a hypothesis, measured by the lapse-recovery and 90-day numbers — not as a fact.
2. **The app can't fix a bad plan.** Vague or oversized units kill motivation regardless of scheduling. The template library needs editorial quality control; the coach should flag units over 90 minutes.
3. **Notification fatigue.** Two pushes a day maximum, adaptive timing, quiet hours. If notification→start conversion drops below 15%, reduce frequency rather than rewording.
4. **Low ARPU in Pakistan.** PKR 499/month × realistic conversion won't fund the company; it funds a lean solo product. International pricing and institutional seats are where margin lives. Plan the cost structure accordingly — especially coach token costs.
5. **Store policy on external payments** is a moving target. Design entitlements to be source-agnostic and keep iOS strictly in-app.
6. **Commitment deposits** involve holding money — payment licensing and charity partnerships need legal review in each market before launch. Ship it last.
7. **Scope creep is the real killer** for a solo builder with a day job. The MVP is four screens and one loop. Everything in Phase 2 and 3 waits for the MVP success criterion.
